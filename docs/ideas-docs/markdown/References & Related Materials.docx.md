"I have a PhD from a top school in a complex, highly theoretical discipline. Approving of the idea to make science more accessible to the masses, I trundled off to Wikipedia and set up information relevant to my field. It was edited out and defaced by typical internet know-it-alls who replaced discussions of cutting-edge research with truisms they found on Ask.com. There were thousands of them and one of me. They had spent all of 15 minutes research the topic on Google, I had spent 10 years and a dissertation on it.

I haven't been back. And actually, none of my colleagues have either.

Knowledge isn't democratic - until the internet figures that out, it's only helping to drag us further into the dark ages."

(AK-47) This guy gets it, people aren't interested in concise just-the-facts traditional encyclopaedia-type entries like that Citizendium example, they want big agglomerations of less-reliable, less-objective information that are more like magazine pieces, like the Wikipedia article.

Please read closely as I list again, I’m judging it on three things:
1) dearth of information, the top of the Wiki page has the summary, the subsections (if you are on a mobile platform they probably aren’t even open by default) are the [optional] extra detail…yes, sometimes I just stop at the header
2) not pointing me towards its underlying data sources so I can better judge the veracity of its [meager] assertions
3) not pointing me towards its underlying data sources so I can go learn even more

P.S. Point #2 especially makes me very uneasy about its reliability, because I take the potential abuse for Appeal to Authority seriously. Lack of transparency is the selling point? Little to no option to drill down for more detailed info is the selling point? WTF?

Its not suppose to be a belief but yet, most people, even scientists, believe in evolution.
