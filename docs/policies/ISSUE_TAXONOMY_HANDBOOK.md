# Issue Taxonomy Handbook

Version: `1.0` (`2026-07-13`)

## Classification Rule

Select the narrowest issue type that describes the review defect. Severity describes impact, not disagreement. A critical issue blocks new discussion and final factual verdicts only after screening acceptance and only on its direct target.

| Category | Use when | Typical outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Incorrect or unsupported | A material factual statement lacks support or conflicts with stronger evidence | Request evidence, change request, or factual verdict review |
| Misleading context | Individual facts may be accurate but framing omits material context | Add context, narrow the claim, or mark mixed evidence |
| Duplicate | Another record expresses the same claim in the same scope | Review deterministic candidates and merge with a reason |
| Wrong type or parent | The content belongs to another entry family or context | Convert or move while preserving history |
| Source or provenance | Evidence identity, integrity, access, or capture details are insufficient | Add provenance or require corroboration |
| Privacy or safety | Content exposes unnecessary personal data or creates a credible safety risk | Restrict visibility, redact through revision, or reject |
| Writing quality | The claim is too broad, ambiguous, or encyclopedic to review | Return with a concise rewrite request |

## Severity

- `Warning`: quality is reduced, but ordinary review can continue.
- `Critical`: proceeding could validate materially false, unsafe, or unreviewable content.

Screeners must not mark ordinary disagreement as critical. Reviewers resolving or dismissing critical issues must cite the corrected revision, evidence, or governing rule.
