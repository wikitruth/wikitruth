# System Overview and Principles

## Mission

Wikitruth is positioned as a structured truth-seeking platform for high-impact, controversial, or confusing topics where users need:

- traceable claims,
- explicit evidence chains,
- and transparent review outcomes.

The recurring intent across source docs is to help users quickly distinguish:

- verified claims,
- unresolved claims,
- and claims with significant issues.

## Scope Boundaries

The system is not intended to be a generic encyclopedia dump.

Primary scope:

- issue-focused truth validation,
- argument decomposition,
- evidence-first discussion,
- and accountability workflows.

Non-goals:

- maximizing content volume without quality controls,
- unstructured social feeds,
- trust-by-authority alone.

## Core Principles

1. Evidence and reasoning over reputation
- Claim quality is derived from support and review process, not author status alone.

2. Structured decomposition
- Large topics are broken into small discussable units for clearer review and reduced ambiguity.

3. Context preservation
- Claims should be evaluated within parent topic context to avoid decontextualized interpretations.

4. No uncontrolled duplication
- Similar/duplicate entries should be merged or blocked to prevent repeated low-value debates.

5. Separation of factual and moral evaluation
- Factual truth and ethical/moral judgments should be represented distinctly.

6. Transparent lifecycle
- Contributions, reviews, verdict changes, and disputes should be traceable over time.

7. Reader-first comprehension
- Content should be concise, navigable, and understandable by non-expert readers.

## Truth Model (Normalized)

The source corpus repeatedly implies a multi-level truth model rather than binary truth.

Normalized interpretation:

- `unreviewed`: newly contributed, not yet screened.
- `screened`: basic quality checks passed.
- `reviewed`: subjected to deeper issue/verdict review.
- `verified`: sufficiently supported under current evidence.
- `partial`: mixed or incomplete support.
- `disputed/issues`: unresolved major concerns.
- `outdated/archived`: no longer current or superseded.

## Content Prioritization Heuristic

Suggested order of work based on repeated notes:

1. High-impact social topics with strong public confusion.
2. Topics with direct quality-of-life implications (health, governance, safety).
3. Topics with recurrent manipulation/propaganda patterns.
4. Lower-impact general knowledge only when needed for context.

## Quality and Safety Guardrails

- Keep claims concise and minimally ambiguous.
- Require issue tagging for logical, terminology, or evidence flaws.
- Avoid ad-hominem and abusive participation patterns.
- Require stronger evidence standards for higher-controversy topics.
- Treat emotionally charged civic allegations as unverified inputs unless evidentially supported.
